Emergency ID Tags for Children in India: A Parent's Guide

calendar_today18 Jan 2026 schedule5 min read sellChild safety

A crowded market, a school trip, a busy railway platform — it takes seconds for a small child to slip out of sight. And when a child is too young, too frightened, or too hurt to say who they are, a stranger who wants to help has no way to reach you. A simple emergency ID tag for children closes that gap.

Why children get lost — or hurt — more often than we think

India's public spaces are wonderfully crowded, and that's exactly the problem. Melas, temple queues, metro stations, wedding halls and beaches all create the perfect conditions for a child to wander a few metres and vanish into a wall of legs. Young children rarely know a full phone number, may not pronounce a surname clearly, and often freeze when approached by an unfamiliar adult.

It isn't only about getting lost. A playground fall, a bee sting, or a sudden reaction on a school outing can turn into a medical situation where a teacher or bystander urgently needs to know one thing: is this child allergic to anything, and who do I call?

Children with special needs, speech delays, or conditions like autism can find these moments even harder to navigate. They may not respond to their own name, may not tolerate being approached, and can struggle to communicate distress at all. For these families especially, a tag isn't a nice-to-have — it's the calm, reliable voice that explains the situation the moment it's needed.

A frightened child may not remember your number. A tag remembers it for them.

What to put on a child's emergency tag

The goal is to give a helpful adult everything they need in one glance, without over-sharing. For a child's medical ID, we recommend keeping it short and specific. Our full breakdown of what information should be on a medical ID tag goes deeper, but for kids the essentials are:

  • First name (a nickname is fine — enough for someone to speak to the child gently)
  • Two parent or guardian contacts — a primary and a backup number
  • Blood group and any critical allergies (peanuts, penicillin, insect stings)
  • Ongoing conditions — asthma, epilepsy, diabetes — and any daily medication
  • A short note like "Please call my parents, I may be lost"

Deliberately leave off your child's full name, home address, and school name. A stranger doesn't need those to help — and keeping them off the tag protects your child's privacy. With SafeTag, contact numbers are revealed only when someone taps to reveal them, so casual scanners never see plain digits.

Where kids should carry it

The best tag is the one that's actually on the child. Good options include:

  • Clipped inside the school bag — for the daily commute and school trips
  • On a wristband for toddlers who won't keep a keychain
  • Pinned to a jacket or shoe during winter travel
  • On a water bottle or lunch bag they always carry

School bags and everyday routine

The school run is where a tag earns its place quietly, every single day. Teachers, bus staff and other parents all carry phones — so if your child is unwell on the bus or gets separated on an excursion, the adult nearest them can reach you in seconds instead of waiting for the school office to find a record.

Festivals, fairs and travel

Festival season is when families are most out-and-about and most at risk of getting separated in a crush of people. We wrote a dedicated guide to festival and travel safety in a crowd — but the short version is: before you step into any large gathering, make sure the youngest members of your group are wearing a tag. It's the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy for peace of mind.

Peace of mind that fits in a pocket

You can't hold a child's hand every second of every day — through school gates, birthday parties and Diwali crowds.
